## Final answer

I compared January–August 2026 with January–August 2025, since the data runs to 31 August 2026. Sales are up slightly, but profit is well down: gross profit fell and costs grew faster than revenue.

## Headline (ERP, DKK)

| | Jan–Aug 2025 | Jan–Aug 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 113.2M | 115.7M | **+2.3%** |
| Gross profit | 45.3M | 42.9M | **−5.3%** |
| Gross margin | 40.0% | 37.1% | −3.0 pts |
| Personnel costs | 19.5M | 21.9M | +12.0% |
| Other operating costs | 9.7M | 10.4M | +6.6% |
| **Operating profit** | **16.0M** | **10.6M** | **−33.6%** |
| Operating margin | 14.2% | 9.2% | −5.0 pts |

The 5.4M drop in operating profit breaks down as −2.4M gross profit, −2.35M personnel costs and −0.64M other operating costs.

## What's driving it

**1. Bigger discounts, mainly on Equipment in Germany and Denmark** (ERP invoice lines)
- The average discount rose from 4.8% to 7.5%. On Equipment it rose from 7.0% to 11.2%, and Equipment's margin fell from 28.4% to 25.4%.
- **Germany Equipment:** discount went from 7.0% to 15.3% and margin from 28.4% to 21.7%. Gross profit fell 1.1M even though revenue was only down 5%.
- **Denmark Equipment:** revenue grew strongly (+28%, +8.2M), but the discount went from 6.9% to 10.8%. Gross profit still grew by 1.25M.
- My rough estimate: at 2025 discount levels, Equipment would have earned about 2.9M more, all else equal.

**2. Denmark Service revenue fell 23% (−4.3M revenue, −2.55M gross profit), and I can't yet explain why**
- This is the largest single hit to gross profit. The margin itself held (59%); the volume is what dropped.
- It's spread across service agreements, technician hours and call-out fees, each down about 24.5%. Many customers are affected.
- It starts in January 2026. Q4 2025 was still at 2025 levels. In the quarterly billing months (Jan/Apr/Jul), 54/68/61 customers were invoiced, against 75/72/66 a year earlier.
- **This doesn't match the CRM.** Service contract renewals look healthy: 16 of 19 won (84%) against 12 of 14 (86%) last year, and won value is up.
  - One customer that renewed, Fjord Køleteknik, still shows 40% less service revenue in the ERP.
  - The number of Danish service technicians is flat at 8, so a staffing gap doesn't obviously explain it either.
- **Interpretation, not established:** this could be a real drop in how much customers are using us, or a billing gap. It should be checked with Finance and Service.

**3. Germany: much higher sales cost, weaker results** (payroll + CRM)
- The German sales team grew from 8 to 12 people (4 hires between Sep 2025 and Jan 2026). Their cost rose 1.77M (+49%), which is most of the total personnel increase. Other departments rose only 2–4%.
- New pipeline in Germany rose 35%, but the win rate fell from 29.9% to 18.1%. It has fallen every quarter, from 32.5% in Q1 2025 to about 17% in Q2 and Q3 2026. Won deal value is down 24%.
- It isn't only new hires still learning the ropes. Several established salespeople's win rates also fell sharply, e.g. from 47% to 23% and from 30% to 13%.
- The most common recorded reasons for losing deals were "price too high" (21%) and "lost to competitor" (19%), even with the deeper discounts. 23% of lost deals have no reason recorded.
- In the ERP, German revenue is down 8% and gross profit down 16%.

**4. Other operating costs:** travel (+26%), marketing and trade fairs (+23%) and car costs (+19%) each rose about 0.2M. Recruitment costs fell 0.31M.

## What's working
- **Denmark:** revenue +7%. The CRM win rate is steady (34% → 35%) and won value is up 16%. Components are also growing.
- **Sweden:** revenue +2%, gross profit +5.5%, and margin improved to 40.8%. Discounts are stable.

## Caveats
- I haven't separated out currency effects. Euro invoicing (Germany) should barely matter because the krone is pegged to the euro. The Swedish krona could affect Swedish figures somewhat.
- The CRM and ERP use their own exchange rates, so values differ slightly between them.
- The causes above come from patterns in the data. Nothing I checked shows *why* discounts rose or why German win rates fell.

## Suggested next steps
1. Reconcile Danish service contracts in the CRM with ERP invoicing for Q1–Q3 2026, starting with the largest declines (Køge Bygningsservice, Fjord Køleteknik, Herning Foder).
2. Review who approves discounts on Equipment deals in Germany and Denmark.
3. Review the German sales expansion's return so far (+1.8M cost against −3.0M won value), and get loss reasons recorded on every lost deal.
