## Final answer

Two customers account for all of the German decline: **Rheinmark Chemie** and **Brandauer Papierwerke**. Together they lost 4.76M DKK of revenue, about twice Germany's net drop of 2.29M DKK. New customers made up part of the difference.

I've taken "the German decline" to mean Jan–Aug 2026 against Jan–Aug 2025, the latest data available. German revenue fell 8.0% (from 28.73M to 26.45M DKK) and gross profit fell 16% (from 11.14M to 9.35M DKK). Revenue figures come from ERP invoices; the reasons behind them come from the CRM.

**How the net change breaks down (ERP):**
- Rheinmark and Brandauer: **−4.76M**
- All other customers who already bought in 2025: −0.66M, the net of large ups and downs
- Customers first invoiced in 2026 (12 of them, led by Wasserwerke Wetterau, Vogtland Stahl and Hunsrück Kunststoffe): **+3.13M**
- Total: −2.29M

The same two customers also account for −2.07M of gross profit, more than Germany's whole gross profit decline of −1.79M.

### 1–2. Rheinmark and Brandauer: contracts lost to competitors
- **When it started:** both fell sharply from Q1 2026. Rheinmark went from 1.27–1.62M DKK a quarter in 2025 to 0.55–0.63M. Brandauer went from 1.01–1.52M to 0.38–0.57M.
- **Rheinmark (CRM):** three deals were lost:
  - The 2026 service renewal, worth 1.03M DKK, was lost to a competitor on 9 Dec 2025.
  - The 2026 spare-parts framework (0.78M) was lost on 30 Jan 2026, with the reason given as "Price too high".
  - The tank-farm pump replacement (0.41M) was lost on 20 Feb 2026 "to the provider that took over the service contract".

  In the ERP, Rheinmark's service revenue fell 0.89M, components 1.04M, and equipment went from 0.67M to zero.
- **Brandauer (CRM):** the 2026 spare-parts framework (0.67M) was lost to a competitor on 22 Jan 2026. The CRM note says Brandauer "moved PM2/PM3 spare parts to a local distributor with consignment stock". In the ERP, its components revenue fell 1.71M (−70%).
- **Handover (CRM):** a sales manager moved both accounts off their long-standing rep, Stefan Brenner, in Sept/Oct 2025 as part of a "DACH territory re-cut". Afterwards:
  - Rheinmark's maintenance contact "asked why Stefan no longer covers the site; wants continuity", and "slow response in Q3" was given as a reason for not renewing.
  - At Brandauer, purchasing "had not been informed about the change of contact person".

  **This is my reading of the notes, not a proven cause.** Nine other accounts were reassigned in the same re-cut and most of them grew, including Lebensmittel Donautal (+1.33M), which also came from Stefan Brenner. So the handover coincides with these two losses, alongside a competitor offering service and spare parts together. It doesn't explain the German decline on its own.

### 3–7. One-off 2025 projects, mostly not repeated
These customers bought mainly equipment in H1 2025 (I checked each one's product categories) and have bought little since. Together they are −3.05M, which is roughly matched by the +3.13M from new 2026 project customers. They look like normal project ups and downs rather than lost customers.

### Worth noting
- **The full-year gap is likely to get bigger (a projection).** Rheinmark and Brandauer were still buying at normal levels in Sep–Dec 2025, so comparisons for those months will look worse. Together they billed about 10.4M DKK in 2025; at the 2026 pace they would bill about 4M.
- **Discounting is a separate problem.** German equipment discounts rose from 7.0% to 15.3% and equipment margin fell from 28% to 22%. That hurts gross profit regardless of which customers are buying.
- **Currency:** I didn't separate out exchange-rate effects. The euro is pegged to the krone, so I don't expect them to matter much.
