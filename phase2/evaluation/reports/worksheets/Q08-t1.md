## Final answer

Denmark's revenue is growing, but its gross profit is falling. Year-to-date (Jan–Aug 2026 vs. Jan–Aug 2025), revenue is up 7%, while gross profit is down 4% and gross margin fell 4.3 percentage points. Two things are behind it: bigger discounts on equipment, and service contracts that were not renewed.

**Headline figures (ERP, DKK)**

| DK, Jan–Aug | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 62.6M | 67.0M | **+7.0%** |
| Gross profit | 25.5M | 24.5M | **−4.3%** |
| Gross margin | 40.8% | 36.5% | −4.3 pp |
| Average discount | 4.6% | 7.4% | +2.9 pp |

Denmark is 58% of group revenue. Sweden's gross profit rose 5.5% on a stable margin, so Sweden is doing better on profitability.

**What's going well**
- **Equipment sales volume is strong.** Equipment revenue rose 28% (29.4M → 37.6M). Measured at list price, volume is up about 33%.
- **The sales pipeline is healthy (CRM, renewals excluded).** The win rate held at 35% (vs. 34%), won deal value rose 16% to 14.4M, new pipeline rose 6%, and open pipeline at end of August was up 17% at 21.9M.
- **11 new customers** were first invoiced in 2026 (ERP).
- **Costs are under control.** Headcount is flat at 33, payroll is up 2.7%, and operating costs booked to DK are up 5.7%.

**Problem 1: Equipment discounting**
- Discounts on DK equipment jumped from about 7.0% to about 10.5–11% in Q1 2026 and have stayed there. Equipment margin fell from about 28.5% to about 25.5% over the same period.
- It affects almost every product, with discounts up 3–5 pp on each of the top 20. Unit costs haven't changed, so the margin loss comes from pricing, not higher costs or a change in product mix.
- My estimate: at last year's discount level, DK would have about **1.6M more gross profit** year-to-date.
- Germany shows the same pattern (discount 7.0% → 15.3%), but Sweden doesn't (flat at about 6.9%).
- I found nothing in CRM notes that explains the change. The cause is unconfirmed.

**Problem 2: Lost service contracts**
- Service revenue fell 23% (−4.3M), which cost **2.55M of gross profit**. Service is DK's highest-margin category at about 59%.
- The losses trace to the December 2025 renewal round (CRM). Only **23 of 39** contracts renewed, and **3.7M of annual contract value was lost (39%)**.
- By comparison, Sweden renewed 92% and Germany 76% over January 2025–August 2026.
- The recorded reasons for lost DK renewals since July 2025 (18 deals, 4.3M) are:
  - "Customer insourced maintenance": 7 deals, 2.3M
  - "Price too high": 4 deals, 1.0M
  - Lost to competitor: 4 deals, 0.55M
  - Other or not recorded: the rest
- Customers such as Køge Bygningsservice, Herning Foder and Lolland Emballage show matching service revenue drops of 40–94% in the ERP.
- The 2026 renewals decided so far went better: 9 of 11 renewed (82%). However, both losses were "price too high".

**Putting it together:** Equipment added 1.25M of gross profit and Components 0.2M, but Service lost 2.55M, for a net of −1.1M.

**Caveats**
- I can't give a reliable Danish operating profit. DK payroll includes group functions (management, finance, IT, marketing), and the ERP payroll journal isn't split by market.
- The group's operating profit fell 34% (from 16.0M to 10.6M), mostly because of Germany. German payroll rose 1.8M (+40%) with 4 more people, and German gross profit fell 1.8M. Denmark is not the main cause of the group's decline.
- The CRM's DKK conversions differ slightly from the ERP's.
- Loss reasons are whatever sales staff selected, and two are blank.

**Worth acting on**
1. Find out who approved the higher equipment discounts from Q1 2026 onward, and whether that was a deliberate decision. It's happening in Denmark and Germany but not Sweden.
2. Prepare for the December 2026 renewal round now. Last year's renewal deals were created in October, and there are no open DK renewal deals in the CRM yet. All DK renewals are handled by one person, Line Østergaard. That's a fact about how the work is set up, not evidence about why contracts were lost.
3. Look into the customers who moved maintenance in-house. That was the biggest single loss reason, and it may be a market trend rather than something the sales team can fix.
