## Final answer

# Did the German sales hiring pay off?

**Not yet.** Twelve months in, Germany is spending more on sales and making less gross profit. The new hires have built a lot of pipeline but closed little of it. The biggest single problem was how existing accounts were handed to them.

## What was invested (payroll)
- Four sales hires: Lukas Hoffmann (Sep 2025), Jana Richter (Oct 2025), Timo Neumann (Nov 2025) and Deniz Yılmaz (Jan 2026). None has left.
- Their combined employer cost through Aug 2026 is **2.23M DKK**.
- German sales payroll for Jan–Aug 2026 was **+1.77M DKK (+49%)** on the same months of 2025, as the team grew from 8 to 12.

## What came back (Jan–Aug 2026 vs Jan–Aug 2025)

| Metric | 2025 | 2026 | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany revenue | 28.7M | 26.4M | **−8.0%** | ERP |
| Germany gross profit | 11.1M | 9.4M | **−16%** (−1.79M) | ERP |
| Germany gross margin | 38.8% | 35.4% | −3.4 pts | ERP |
| Average discount, Germany | 5.2% | 9.7% | +4.5 pts | ERP |
| Denmark / Sweden revenue | | | +7% / +2% | ERP |

- Gross profit fell 1.79M while sales payroll rose 1.77M. In 8 months, Germany's gross profit after sales payroll is about **3.5M DKK worse**.
- The drop is specific to Germany, since Denmark and Sweden grew. Denmark did also see higher discounts and a lower margin, so some of the margin pressure may be company-wide.

## Why

**1. Much more selling activity, but no more business won (CRM, Sep 2025–Aug 2026 vs prior 12 months)**
- New pipeline in H1 2026 was 35.1M DKK, up 35% on H1 2025.
- Closed deals rose 70% (135 to 230), but the value won was flat (13.9M vs 13.6M).
- The German win rate fell from **30% to 20%**. Denmark (about 34%) and Sweden (about 30%) held steady.
- **New hires:** 8 wins out of 69 closed deals (12%), worth 2.65M DKK. That is barely above their 2.23M cost, and gross profit on it would be well below. Their equipment wins carried 15–20% discounts.
- **Existing reps:** their win rate also fell, to 24%. So the drop isn't only a new-hire effect.

**2. Moving existing accounts to the new hires was costly (CRM owner history + ERP)**
- Between Sep and Nov 2025, the German country manager, Andreas Keller, moved 13 existing accounts from experienced reps to the new hires in bulk.
- Revenue from those 13 accounts fell **12.1M → 9.1M DKK (−25%)**. Revenue from all other German customers rose **+4.6%**.
- Two accounts, both previously handled by Stefan Brenner, explain most of this:
  - **Rheinmark Chemie: −2.6M.** CRM notes say the customer asked why Stefan no longer covered them. They then dropped their service contract (a 1.03M DKK renewal lost to a competitor), and a pump replacement project also went to the competitor.
  - **Brandauer Papierwerke: −2.2M.** CRM notes say purchasing was "not informed" of the change of contact. The customer then moved its spare parts to a local distributor.
- **Caution:** the Rheinmark note also cites a competitor's bundled offer and slow response in Q3 2025, which was before the handover. Some other moved accounts grew, e.g. Lebensmittel Donautal +1.3M. So the handover is a strong contributing factor, not the only cause.

**3. Some new business, at lower margins (ERP)**
- 19 German customers were invoiced for the first time from Sep 2025. They brought 5.6M DKK revenue at a 26% gross margin (Germany averaged 38.8% in 2025).
- Only about 1.3M of that revenue can be tied to deals the new hires won.

## Caveats
- **It's early.** B2B industrial sales take time to ramp up, and Deniz has been in the role only 8 months.
- **There is pipeline still to play for.** The new hires hold 12.2M of Germany's 19.0M DKK open pipeline. At their current 12% win rate that would convert to only about 1.5M, but this is a projection, not a result.
- **Sources don't match exactly.** CRM won value is not the same as ERP revenue, and ERP data starts only in Jan 2025, so I can't compare new-customer counts with earlier years.

## Suggested next steps
1. Restore continuity on the largest German accounts, for example joint coverage with the previous rep.
2. Put limits on discounts for German equipment deals.
3. Set ramp-up targets for the new hires (win rate, margin) and review again in Q1 2027, when today's pipeline has closed.
