## Final answer

The increase is mainly headcount: four sales people hired in Germany between September 2025 and January 2026 explain about 74% of it. Pay rises on existing staff explain most of the rest, and exchange rates play almost no part.

**The increase:** Personnel costs for Jan–Aug 2026 were **21.89M DKK, up 2.35M (+12.0%)** on Jan–Aug 2025 (payroll, employer cost). The ERP payroll journal matches payroll almost to the krone each month, so the two systems agree and nothing sits outside payroll. Monthly costs stepped up from about 2.35M to about 2.65M between September 2025 and January 2026 and have stayed there. August 2026 was +12% on August 2025.

**Where the +2.35M came from (payroll)**

| Driver | Change (DKK) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 4 new German sales hires (Hoffmann, Richter, Neumann, Yılmaz) | +1.74M | 74% |
| Denmark, all departments (headcount unchanged) | +0.36M | 15% |
| Sweden (Sales +161k, Service +24k) | +0.19M | 8% |
| Existing German staff | +0.06M | 3% |
| **Total** | **+2.35M** | |

**What the data shows**
1. **German sales headcount went from 8 to 12.** Total headcount was 51 → 54, with 6 hires and 2 departures in the period. The only other hire was a like-for-like replacement of a Danish service technician on the same salary.
2. **German hires cost more per salary krone.** German employer social contributions are about 22% of gross pay, against about 1% in Denmark (where employer pension is about 8%). That is why social contributions rose 28% (+370k), and 311k of that came from the four new hires.
3. **Pay rises took effect in January 2026.** For staff employed in both months, base salary rose 3.6% from August 2025 to August 2026 (Denmark 3.4%, Germany 4.4%, Sweden 3.6%). In Denmark this adds roughly 0.45M, partly offset by lower bonuses.
4. **Bonuses went down, not up.** The March bonus payout was 529k in 2026 against 637k in 2025 (−17%). Commission rose only +100k, and the new hires' commission more than accounts for that.
5. **Exchange rates hardly matter.** Payroll converts EUR at a flat 7.46 in both years. The SEK rate moved from 0.66 to 0.67, worth only about 30k.
6. **Sweden's +161k in Sales** mostly reflects a vacancy in 2025: a sales engineer left in March 2025 and his replacement started in May at a higher salary. Pay rises and the SEK move add the rest.

**Context (CRM and ERP) — correlation, not proven cause**
- German pipeline has grown: deals created are up 35% in value and 38% in number compared with Jan–Aug 2025.
- German results have fallen:
  - Win rate dropped from 29.9% to 18.1%.
  - Won deal value is down 24%.
  - ERP revenue in Germany is down 8% (−2.29M) and gross profit down 16% (−1.79M).
- The four new hires have won 8 of 69 closed deals since September 2025 (11.6%), worth 2.64M. That compares with about 1.74M of employer cost for them in Jan–Aug 2026 alone. Yılmaz has 0 wins from 8 closed deals.

My reading, which the data does not prove: the German sales expansion looks like a deliberate investment that has not paid back yet. New sales people usually take time to ramp up, so the results are not a verdict on the hires. But at about 2.6M DKK a year for the four of them, their conversion rate is worth watching. The CRM does not record why these hires were made or what targets they were given.

**Where the pressure is heading:** the step-up is in fixed salaries, so it is permanent, not a one-off. At current levels, full-year 2026 personnel costs will be about 3.5M above 2025 unless headcount changes.
