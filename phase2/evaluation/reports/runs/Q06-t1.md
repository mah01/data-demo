## Final answer

Mostly existing customers, and mostly two of them. Revenue from new customers held level year on year. New business still has a problem: the German sales pipeline roughly doubled, but the win rate fell and new customers rarely buy again.

**Revenue bridge, Germany, January–August 2026 vs January–August 2025** (ERP invoices, DKK)

| Customer group | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Germany total** | 28.73M | 26.45M | **−2.29M (−8%)** |
| Customers set up before 2025 | 25.56M | 23.07M | −2.48M |
|   – Rheinmark Chemie | 4.11M | 1.51M | −2.60M |
|   – Brandauer Papierwerke | 3.30M | 1.15M | −2.16M |
|   – All other established customers | | | +2.27M |
| New customers set up in 2025 (9) | 3.18M | 0.35M | −2.83M |
| New customers set up since Sep 2025 (13) | – | 3.02M | +3.02M |

- **Existing customers:** the German drop comes down to Rheinmark and Brandauer, which together lost 4.76M. The rest of the established customers grew by 2.27M. Denmark (+7%) and Sweden (+2%) grew over the same period.
- **New customers:** first-year revenue was about the same, 3.18M in 2025 and 3.02M in 2026. But last year's new customers kept only 11% of their revenue this year; five of the nine bought almost nothing. They mostly place one project order and don't come back.

**CRM sales pipeline, Germany, same periods**

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| New business: deals created | 55 deals / 12.9M | 116 deals / 32.5M |
| New business: win rate | 24% | 14% |
| New business: won | 12 deals / 4.42M | 14 deals / 4.20M |
| Existing customers: deals created | 70 deals / 21.9M | 56 deals / 14.5M |
| Existing customers: win rate | 34% | 25% |
| Existing customers: won | 23 deals / 8.00M | 15 deals / 5.18M |

The lower win rates are specific to Germany. Denmark and Sweden held steady or improved (Denmark existing customers 39%→38%, Denmark new business 27%→31%, Sweden new business 32%→36%, Sweden existing customers unchanged at 28%).

**What happened at the two accounts** (CRM ownership history and sales notes)
- Between September and November 2025, 13 German accounts were moved to three newly hired salespeople in a territory re-cut. Both accounts had been handled by Stefan Brenner.
- **Rheinmark:** in June 2025 the customer was "very satisfied with service response times" and wanted a bigger service agreement.
  - After the handover to Lukas Hoffmann in September, the customer asked why Stefan no longer covered the site.
  - In November a competitor offered 24-hour response, and the customer asked for a 12% price cut.
  - In December the service contract renewal (1.03M) went to that competitor, which bundled service and spare parts. The note also cites slow response in Q3. It was the largest German renewal loss.
  - In February 2026 the planned pump replacement project went to the same competitor. Rheinmark revenue fell in all three categories: spare parts −1.04M, service −0.89M, equipment −0.67M.
- **Brandauer** (moved to Jana Richter in October): the first meeting noted that purchasing "had not been informed about the change of contact person." In January 2026 the customer moved its routine spare parts to a local distributor that holds stock on site. Spare-parts revenue fell 70% (−1.71M).

**Interpretation, not proven:** the handovers happened just before these accounts' renewal decisions, and both customers mentioned them. Competitors offering bundles, local stock and lower prices clearly played a part too. The reassignment alone doesn't explain the drop: 8 of the 13 moved accounts grew (Lebensmittel Donautal +1.33M), and without these two the moved accounts were up 1.71M net.

**Margins are also under pressure.** German gross profit fell 16% (−1.79M), and gross margin dropped from 38.8% to 35.4%. Average equipment discounts roughly doubled, from 7.0% to 15.3%. New customers' first orders run at about 17–24% margin.

**Caveats**
- ERP data only starts in January 2025, so "new customer" means one set up in the ERP in 2025 or later.
- 23% of German lost deals have no loss reason recorded, so loss-reason analysis is weak.
- The causes above come from sales notes, not independent checks.

**Worth doing next:** a recovery plan for Rheinmark, which still buys 1.25M a year in spare parts, and for Brandauer. Also check which other key German accounts have service renewals coming up after a change of owner. And look at how German new-business deals are qualified, since twice the pipeline produced the same wins.
