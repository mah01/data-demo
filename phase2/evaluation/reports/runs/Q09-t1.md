## Final answer

If German conversion had stayed at its 2025 level, Jan–Aug 2026 operating profit would have been about **DKK 1.0M higher (range 0.75–1.3M)**. That means about DKK 11.7M instead of the actual 10.6M, and an operating margin of about 9.7% instead of 9.2%. That recovers only about a fifth of the DKK 5.4M year-on-year profit decline, so German conversion is a real factor but not the main one.

## What actually happened

| | Jan–Aug 2025 | Jan–Aug 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company revenue | 113.2M | 115.7M | ERP |
| Company operating profit | 16.0M (14.2%) | 10.6M (9.2%) | ERP |
| German win rate (by deal count) | 28.3% (full year 2025) | 18.1% (29 won of 160 closed) | CRM |
| German win rate (by deal value) | 33.0% | 22.3% | CRM |

The drop is real, not a data artefact. It shows every quarter: 32.5% in Q1 2025, falling to 16.7–17.0% in Q2–Q3 2026. Almost all German deals have an explicit German market tag, and there's no sign of old deals being closed out in bulk.

## How I estimated it

1. **Extra wins.** I kept the 160 German deals that closed in 2026 and applied the 2025 win rate. That gives about 45 wins instead of 29, so about 16 more.
2. **Value of those wins.** I valued them at the average lost German deal (DKK 250k), which gives **DKK 4.1M** in extra won deal value.
   - A second method keeps each deal type (new vs existing customers) and product category at its own 2025 rate. It gives 3.0M.
   - Valuing the extra wins at the average won deal gives 5.3M.
3. **Won deals become invoices.** I traced sample deals into the ERP. They were invoiced at about their deal value within 1–2 months (e.g. a €101.7k deal was followed by €103.4k invoiced).
4. **Profit on that revenue.** I applied 2026 German gross margins by category, weighted towards Equipment (73% of lost deal value). That gives about 28%. I also deducted about 4% for sales commission including employer contributions, based on the 2026 German commission paid relative to won value in payroll.

| Scenario (Jan–Aug 2026) | Actual | With 2025 German conversion |
|---|---|---|
| German revenue | 26.4M | ~30.5M (Jan–Aug 2025: 28.7M) |
| German gross profit | 9.4M | ~10.5M (Jan–Aug 2025: 11.1M) |
| Company operating profit | 10.6M | **~11.7M (range 11.4–11.9M)** |
| Operating margin | 9.2% | **~9.7%** |

The timing and follow-on effects roughly offset each other. Deals won in July–August would partly be invoiced after August, which makes the figure a bit high. Follow-up spare-parts and service sales to newly won customers aren't counted, which makes it a bit low.

## What else drove the decline

The following comes straight from the data:
- **German equipment is being sold at deeper discounts.** The average discount rose from 7.0% to 15.3% and gross margin fell from 28.4% to 21.7% (ERP). That costs about DKK 0.9M of gross profit on this year's sales, about the same as the conversion effect. It's also why German gross profit stays below last year even in the scenario.
- **German sales payroll is up DKK 1.8M (+49%)** after four hires between September 2025 and January 2026 (payroll). This is more than the conversion shortfall.
- **Denmark's gross margin fell from 40.8% to 36.5%,** with average discount up from 4.6% to 7.4%. That is a DKK 1.1M gross-profit drop despite revenue growing 7% (ERP).

My reading of the German decline, which the data suggests but doesn't prove:
- **The new hires pull the rate down,** at 8 wins from 67 closed deals (12%). But the existing team also slipped, from 29.9% to 22.6%, so it isn't only a ramp-up effect.
- **The deal mix has shifted.** More of the 2026 deals are with companies that aren't yet customers (63% vs 43%), and those convert less often. On the second method above, this explains about 2.5 of the 10-point drop.
- **Pipeline quality may have fallen.** New German pipeline rose 35% in the first half of 2026. "Not qualified" and "No response" appear as loss reasons only in 2026, and more lost deals have no reason recorded (23% vs 16%).

Restoring German conversion alone wouldn't restore profitability. Discounting in Germany and Denmark and the added German sales cost matter at least as much.
