## Final answer

# Why the German pipeline is growing while German revenue falls

The two numbers measure different things. The pipeline growth comes from new-customer deals opened by four new German sales reps, and few of those deals are being won. The revenue decline comes from existing customers, mainly two large accounts that moved to new reps in a territory re-cut in autumn 2025 and then reduced their spend sharply.

## Both trends are real (Jan–Aug 2026 vs Jan–Aug 2025)

| Metric | 2025 | 2026 | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| German revenue | 28.73M DKK | 26.45M DKK | **−8.0%** | ERP |
| German gross profit | 11.14M DKK | 9.35M DKK | −16% | ERP |
| New German pipeline (deals opened) | 34.8M DKK (125 deals) | 46.9M DKK (172 deals) | **+35%** | CRM |
| Won value (deals closed as won) | 12.4M DKK | 9.4M DKK | −24% | CRM |
| Win rate (share of closed deals won) | 29.9% | 18.1% | −12 pts | CRM |

Denmark and Sweden don't show this pattern; Denmark's revenue grew about 9.5% in H1.

## 1. The pipeline growth is new-customer deals from four new reps, and they rarely close

**What the data shows:**
- **Split by deal type:** pipeline for new customers rose from 10.6M to 32.1M DKK. Pipeline for existing customers fell from 21.0M to 13.6M DKK (−35%).
- **Who created it:** payroll shows four sales hires between Sep 2025 and Jan 2026: Hoffmann, Richter, Neumann and Yilmaz. In the CRM they opened 95 deals worth 25.1M DKK, about 53% of all German pipeline created in 2026. Most tenured reps opened 40–60% less pipeline than a year earlier.
- **How it converts:**
  - The new reps won 8 of 67 closed deals (12%).
  - New-customer deals overall were won 14% of the time.
  - Tenured reps' existing-customer deals were won 29% of the time.
- **Loss reasons:** "Not qualified" appears as a new reason (15 deals), and the share of losses with no reason recorded rose from 15% to 23%.

**My interpretation:** the pipeline is bigger because more low-probability deals are being opened, not because more business is being won.

**I also checked two other explanations and ruled them out:**
- **Wrong country tagging:** nearly all German deals have the country set on the deal itself, so they aren't misfiled from other markets.
- **Old deals left open:** only 6 of 66 open deals are past their expected close date.

## 2. Revenue fell mainly in accounts moved during the territory re-cut

**The reassignment:** CRM owner history shows the German sales manager (Andreas Keller) moved 13 German accounts from tenured reps to the new hires in three batches (15 Sep, 6 Oct and 10 Nov 2025).

**What those accounts bought (ERP):** revenue fell from 12.14M to 9.09M DKK, a drop of 3.05M (−25%). That is larger than the whole German decline. Two accounts drive it:

- **Rheinmark Chemie: −2.60M DKK (−63%).** Service fell 0.89M, spare parts 1.04M and equipment 0.67M.
  - The CRM notes record the customer asking "why Stefan no longer covers the site" and wanting continuity.
  - The 2026 service contract renewal (1.03M DKK) was lost to a competitor that bundled service with spare parts; "slow response in Q3" was cited.
  - A later pump project went to the provider that took over the service contract.
- **Brandauer Papierwerke: −2.16M DKK (−65%)**, mostly spare parts (−1.71M).
  - The CRM notes say the customer's purchasing contact "had not been informed" about the change of rep.
  - In Jan 2026 the customer moved its spare parts to a local distributor.

Not every reassigned account declined. Lebensmittel Donautal grew 1.33M DKK and Lausitz Chemie grew 0.42M.

## 3. Why the rest of the revenue picture doesn't offset this

**German revenue bridge (Jan–Aug 2026 vs 2025):**

| Group | Change |
|---|---|
| The 13 reassigned accounts | −3.05M DKK |
| Other existing customers | −2.40M DKK |
| Customers first invoiced since Sep 2025 | +3.16M DKK |
| **Total** | **−2.29M DKK** |

- **Other existing customers:** this drop is mostly large one-off equipment orders in 2025 that didn't repeat. I haven't investigated those further.
- **New customers:** they buy mostly equipment at about 22% gross margin. The spare parts and service revenue that was lost earned 42–59%. That is why gross profit fell twice as fast as revenue.
- **Discounts:** the average discount on German equipment rose from about 7% to about 16%, and margin fell from 28% to 21%. The ERP doesn't record which rep made each sale, so I can't link this to the new hires. Denmark's discounts also rose, though less.

**Structural reason (interpretation):** most of the lost revenue was recurring spare parts and service. That kind of revenue barely shows up as new pipeline, while new equipment deals do. So pipeline can grow while recurring revenue drains away.

## Caveats
- The link between the re-cut and customer losses is directly documented only for Rheinmark and Brandauer. For the other 11 accounts it's a correlation, and the sample is small.
- I haven't checked what happened to the reps who gave up these accounts, or why the re-cut was done.

## Suggested next steps
- **Change the headline measure:** track Germany on won value and pipeline weighted by probability of closing, not on pipeline opened.
- **Win back the two accounts:** a plan for Rheinmark's service contract and Brandauer's spare parts.
- **Review the re-cut:** check how handovers were done for the largest reassigned accounts.
- **Coach the new reps:** focus on qualifying deals before opening them.
- **Review discounts:** look at equipment discount levels and approvals.
